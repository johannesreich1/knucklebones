What it says: it changed hands. The die does not travel — it comes apart there and re-forms here, which is the only idea in this set with no path between two rectangles.
That is an engineering argument, not a taste one. Everything else here has to be re-authored when the board moves: portrait to landscape swaps which axis separates the halves, face-to-face turns one half upside down, and the widget renders inside its own containing block (ui/fx pin() exists entirely because of that). A transfer with no trajectory is indifferent to all of it, and it is the only one that stays correct if the board layout is ever changed again.
It also degrades cleanly. Under prefers-reduced-motion it becomes a cross-fade and still says the same sentence; the path-based ideas have nothing left when their path is removed.
Cost — and it is about meaning, not pixels: dissolving is the game's language for destruction. .die.dying already scales a die away with a brightness flash, and a stolen die that pops into sparks on their board looks exactly like a die that was just killed there — on a rune whose entire distinction from a strike is that nothing was destroyed. It needs a hue and a curve that read as "gone somewhere", not "gone", and the gap between those two is narrow.
