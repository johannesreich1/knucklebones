What it says: the column is full of the protection, not ringed by it. Nothing here is an edge, so nothing here can be lost against a die that already carries a border and a bloom — this is the one option of the six you read from across the room, and the only one that answers "which columns are guarded?" without the player looking for a line. Half the volume sits behind the dice where it can run hot, a thin veil sits in front so the dice are standing in it, and three motes drift at 9–13s — weather, not an alert. The drop is the mist getting out of the way: it billows off the floor, two puffs are shouldered aside, and the volume folds back in behind the die. The die itself falls exactly as it falls into an unguarded column, because a ward eats a STRIKE and never refuses your own placement. The absorb goes solid for four frames, then thins to two thirds and stays thin — a spent ward is less mist, so the charge reads without a number. Cost: this is the loudest option at rest, and it is the one that changes what the board looks like. The front veil is real tint over real pips — 15% at the floor, 0% at the ceiling — and it can only get quieter, never louder, without the bottom die going soft. Two adjacent guarded columns pool into one cloud unless the gutter grows. And the board already has a background wash and a bloom per die; a mode where every full column fogs up is a board that is mostly weather.
