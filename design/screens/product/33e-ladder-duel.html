A ladder is a list of people to beat, so the tap says exactly that. The board itself stays the leanest of the eight — rank, die, name, points, and the group compressed to a glowing material dot — because this study spends its budget on the detail: tapping any player deals a face-off card, their die against yours over the VS mark the matchmaking reveal already owns, then the same five facts stated twice and mirrored around the centre. You leave knowing the answer to the question you actually asked — "how do I stack up against them?" — not just their numbers. The gap line names the distance and nothing else: what a match would pay is never previewed anywhere in this app, because that number depends on the opponent and would be wrong half the time. Costs: the dot-only group reading is the subtlest of the study, and a compare against rank 1 from rank 200 reads as a wall — which is honest, but cold.
