What it does: nothing sits in the card's corners. A 40×4 bar rides its top edge — the mark every native sheet wears to say drag me away — and dismissal becomes a downward drag: the card follows the finger, the wash lightens as it travels (at 152px it is a quarter of its weight and the ladder reads through it), and past 96px the release sends it out. Short of that it springs home and nothing happened. Backdrop tap and Escape are untouched — this is a third way out, not a replacement for the two that already exist. What it buys: the top of the card is symmetric again, nothing hovers over the gold column's avatar, and the affordance costs 6px of height and no width (14px wrapper, −8 into the card's padding, −12 cancelling the flex gap, +12 gap back). It answers both retired shapes instead of dodging them: not navigation at the bottom of a screen, and not a button adrift in a band of empty card, because it is centred on an edge rather than parked in a corner. Where it argues with the law: 00-navigation says a sheet leaves by the shared ✕ in its corner, and every other sheet wears one — this would be the one that does not. So it forces a choice: either the face-off is a granted exception (it is already odd, a modal above every overlay rather than a sheet over live context), or the grabber eventually belongs on the reading sheets and both libraries too, which is a far bigger job than this study. And a grabber is conventionally worn by a sheet attached to the bottom edge; ours is centred, so "drag me back where I came from" is a weaker promise here than on a sheet that slid up. What it costs: the bar promises a gesture that must then exist — the mark without the handler is worse than today's ✕. A player who never tries dragging is left with the backdrop, which on a 320px phone is a 14px rail either side of the card (the bands above and below are generous, but nothing says any of it is tappable). And no focusable, announceable way out survives: the keyboard keeps Escape, a screen reader hears nothing. Shipping it means keeping a visually hidden Close, or giving the grabber a button role and a focus ring drawn around the 14px wrapper rather than the 4px bar. To ship it: pointer events on .focard, touch-action:none, setPointerCapture; dy = max(0, y − y0) into the transform and into the wash's alpha through one custom property, so the colour keeps a single home. Commit on dy > 96 or a flick faster than ~0.5px/ms, because a quick short flick that springs back feels stuck. Out in 180ms ease-in, back in 220ms with a small overshoot, and every path calls the existing close(), so gesture, wash tap and Escape stay one implementation. The trap: the pointerup ending a drag can fire the wash's own click and chase a spring-back with an accidental close, so swallow one click after any drag that moved. Reduced motion keeps the follow — it is the player's own finger — and makes the exit instant. test16 stops measuring the ✕'s clearance from the avatar and starts on the bar's painted rect plus two synthesised drags: 120px leaves no .faceoff in the DOM, 40px leaves it there with its transform back at none.
