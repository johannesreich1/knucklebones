The fifth look is the opponent's — idle, in their plate above
When. Your turn, after the roll, before you place — the same gate placement itself uses. The die stays in your hand: a cast is not a move.
Where. Every rune says for itself. FATE, NUDGE and SUNDER land on the die in hand: the columns stand down and the stage is the one thing lit. WARD and ANVIL point at your three columns, PILFER at theirs — and only the columns the spell's own legal() accepts are ringed, which is what stops a ward from advertising the enemy's half. A cast that would buy nothing is refused rather than spent: a second ward on one column, an anvil that would recast a face to itself.
Two jobs, two places. The rune you can cast is a thing you WIELD: full size beside the die in play, a short drag from every column, opposite the LIMITED bag. The opponent's is a readout only — small and inert in their nameplate, immediately left of their score, because the total is the number the eye returns to every turn. Nothing about it reacts to a finger. Making the two identical implied you could cast theirs, and buried the one you can.
Cost. One cast per game — two for FATE. A rune holding more than one charge wears the number it has left; a single charge needs none. A spent rune stays put, greyed: a rune that vanished would read as a bug rather than as a cost. A cast that landed on the die in hand can be pressed again to put it back until the die is played — except FATE, which is final the moment it has shown you the next die.
Chosen, not toggled. The OFFLINE screen picks a spell the way it picks a game mode — the same icon row, each rune slice wearing the mark it wears in the rail, NONE first and default, RANDOM last. RANDOM is not a seventh rune but the same promise the mode row makes, in that row's own mark: the deck then deals it in front of both players (see Rune deal — dealt). Ranked matches (the server replays a plain move log) and the tutorial (a scripted lesson) always deal NONE, and NONE is exactly the table that shipped before spells existed.

The rail, the gestures, the charge accounting and the CSS never learn its name.
