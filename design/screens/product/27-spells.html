When. Your turn, after the roll, before you place — the same gate placement itself uses. The die stays in your hand: a cast is not a move.
Where. Every rune says for itself. FATE, NUDGE and SUNDER land on the die in hand: the columns stand down and the stage is the one thing lit. WARD and ANVIL point at your three columns, PILFER at theirs — and only the columns the spell's own legal() accepts are ringed, which is what stops a ward from advertising the enemy's half. A cast that would buy nothing is refused rather than spent: a second ward on one column, an anvil that would recast a face to itself.
One slot, one hand. The card belongs to whoever is to move. In portrait its centre follows the board's rightmost column while it stays vertically beside the die; landscape centres it above the die. Its rune-coloured surface wash stays quiet and constant — no availability pulse. The die colour and status name the owner, so the card needs no seat mark and the plates need no second readout. It is the reveal card at rail size, not another rune object.
Cost. One cast per game — two for FATE. Every charge is a face-down card at its own tilt. Committing turns the top card once and deals it away; the fully spent hand is the same stack of dashed outlines, holding the stage still. An ordinary aim may be cancelled before a legal target commits it. No committed cast can be put back: self spells commit on the die, WARD and PILFER on their selected legal column, and ANVIL as soon as its weakest-die markings appear. Its committed rune stays locked to that choice until one of the marked full columns is selected.
Chosen, not toggled. The OFFLINE screen picks a spell the way it picks a game mode — the same icon row, each rune slice wearing the mark it wears in the rail, NONE first and default, RANDOM last. RANDOM is not a seventh rune but the same promise the mode row makes, in that row's own mark: the deck then deals it in front of both players (see Rune deal — dealt). Ranked matches (the server replays a plain move log) and the tutorial (a scripted lesson) always deal NONE, and NONE is exactly the table that shipped before spells existed.
Adding one. An object in core/spells.ts — id, name, blurb, detail, aim, target and side, uses, commitsOnAim only when the preview itself commits, previewDieIndex() when one exact die is marked, legal(), apply(), and cpuCast when its value never shows on the boards — plus its path AND its hue in ui/spellicons.ts, and its cast animation in src/flow/spell-effects.ts's EFFECTS (a rune with no entry falls back to defaultEffect). The rail, the gestures, the charge accounting and the CSS never learn its name.
