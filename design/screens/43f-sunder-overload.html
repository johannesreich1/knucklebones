What it says: these are already coming apart. The mark is not applied to the dice, it is the dice — they take the ember through the die's own hue token, so border, pips and bloom change together and the face itself is what looks wrong. Of the six this is the one you cannot miss out of the corner of an eye, because motion catches the eye where colour does not.
Two states, one language. The hand STRAINS (swelling against a ring that is losing) and the board FAILS (crooked, shivering, shedding). Same heat, two different verbs — so which die is the cause and which are the effects needs no arrow between them.
Cost — the honest one: taking --dc means the doomed dice stop wearing their multiplier gold, so the board loses the "these two are a pair" reading exactly where it matters most. It also loses the seat colour, so a face-to-face player glancing over sees ember dice on their opponent's half and no longer at a glance whose they are. And a permanent tremor on up to five dice is a lot of continuous motion sitting on screen for the rest of the turn — a candidate for calming down after the first second, and for skipping entirely under prefers-reduced-motion (ui/fx REDUCED), where an idea built on movement has nothing left to degrade to.
