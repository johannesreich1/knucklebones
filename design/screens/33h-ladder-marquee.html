NEON gets the electricity; everyone else gets calm. The apex is the game's own light, so its holders sit in a lit marquee — glow wash, cyan points, the only luminous thing on the board — while the list below goes quieter than any other variant: the group is a 3px material tick on the row's left edge and nothing more. One glance still answers "where do the materials change", but the screen spends its drama on exactly one thing. The tap deals the player as a collectible card framed in their material — slightly rotated, like a die just thrown — with points, fill, record, streak and peak on its face. In a dice game, a player you're sizing up SHOULD feel like a piece you pick up and turn over. Costs: the marquee is only as tall as the apex is scarce (at 100+ players it may hold 2–3 rows), and the card's theatre would wear thin if it opened on every scroll-tap — it wants a real press, not a graze.
