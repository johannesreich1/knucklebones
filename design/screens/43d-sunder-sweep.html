What it says: watch what is about to happen. The wave is the strike itself, played once at a speed the eye can follow, and the frames it leaves are its receipt. It is the only idea that teaches the rule by performing it — everything else states a conclusion.
Being stopped is a thing only a wave can show. The shielded column breaks it, in the mode's own gold, at the mode's own edge. A per-die mark can only say "spared" by omission; this says it by collision.
The die in hand gets an orbit rather than a pulse, because "every column" is a circle: the ring going all the way round is the same sentence the wave just spoke on the board.
Cost: it is a one-shot beat, so it costs TIME — the cast has to hold the board for the ~600ms the wave takes, on top of a placement the player is already waiting through. And the mark it leaves is a plain ember frame, the weakest of the six at rest: if the player looks away during the sweep, what remains is only slightly louder than today's glow. Worth measuring against a real turn before committing.
