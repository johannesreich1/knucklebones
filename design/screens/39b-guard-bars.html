What it says: the column is fenced, not covered. Every pixel of the protection stands in the gutter, so nothing is laid over a die and pip contrast is exactly what it is on an unguarded column — that is this option's whole argument against W1's pane, and it is why the bars can run at 88% where a ring on the column has to settle for 35%. Rails hold steady, only the caps breathe: one slow thing to catch the eye, none of it moving over the board. The drop is the cap splitting at its seam and swinging open before the die arrives — the die falls between the rails at its own speed and the cap snaps shut behind it with a spark. Nothing ever slows the placement, so the animation cannot tell the lie that a ward refuses your own die. The absorb is that same cap staying shut: rails to full, the enemy die stops dead on it and is thrown back out of frame. Open cap = yours, shut cap = theirs — one shape, both halves of the rule. Cost: it is the thinnest of the six and the easiest to lose. 3px bars can read as table chrome rather than protection, and the frame says the column is guarded without ever saying which dice are in it — the shield reads at the edges, never on the thing it protects. It also spends the gutter: two adjacent guarded columns put four rails into the space between them, and a board where every column is shielded stops being marked columns and becomes a grid.
