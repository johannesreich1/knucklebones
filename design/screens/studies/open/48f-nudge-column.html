What it says: that pip was worth twenty points. Every other idea in this set is about the die; this one is about the reason anybody spent their only charge. A player who sees the third column light up learns what the rune is FOR, which is the thing a first-time caster actually needs.
It uses the game's own vocabulary for value — the triple's heat (--mx3), the column chip, and the floating gain ui/fx already prints on every placement — so nothing new has to be learned to read it.
Cost: it is a preview of a move that has not been made. The cast does not place the die; the player is still free to put that 4 anywhere, and lighting one column comes close to telling them where to play — which the game deliberately does NOT do outside the tutorial (docs: point previews are a lesson feature). It is also wrong the moment the best column is not obvious: the rune's value can be spread across two columns, or defensive, and this idea can only ever name one. And on the wrap it has nothing to say at all, which is why the strip below shows the 6 → 1 case doing exactly what the plain tick does.
