the wrap
What it says: this is what you are about to do. It is the only idea in the set that changes the DECISION rather than the feedback — every other card animates something the player has already committed to. One cast a match, no take-backs, and a wrap that can cost five pips: this is a rune that deserves a preview.
It could not wear the rune's own colour. NUDGE's sky (#7fd7ff) is a neighbour of the default player cyan, so a ghost pip in it disappears into the die it is drawn on — and disappears completely for a player who picked cyan in Settings. The ghost is gold instead, which is already what the game means by "this is the cast you are aiming" (html.castself's ring on this very box), and the wrap's warning is the destruction red.
The wrap becomes a refusal you can make. A red ghost of a 1 over a 6 is the game saying "you can, but look" — and it says it while the choice is still open, which is the only moment the information is worth anything.
The mechanism already exists. A self-aimed cast already lights the stage (html.castself #dieStage in styles/game/spells.css); the ghost hangs on the same state.
Cost: it puts a second face on the die in play, and that die is the single most important object on the screen — anything stacked on it risks being read as the face itself, especially at a glance mid-turn. It is also a precedent: once one rune previews its result, every rune that can is expected to, and SUNDER's or PILFER's preview is a whole board's worth of marks rather than four pips.
