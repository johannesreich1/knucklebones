What it says: how heavy the bag still is — carried by the one event the player watches on every single turn, so it needs no memory and no comparison with an earlier moment.
It costs the bag nothing at rest. The pile, the shells, the fan and the count are pixel-identical to what ships today; this is the only idea in the set that adds no mark at all. It can therefore sit underneath any of the other three rather than competing with them.
Cost — and the reason the first version died. It was proposed as a tempo: idle micro-settles whose interval quickened as the bag emptied. The adversarial pass killed it on perception, not pixels — a rate needs two or three events to exist, and the player's window almost never contains them. The opponent's turn is about seven seconds and the player spends it reading their own board. Moving the signal into one event fixes that, but the honest limits remain: it says nothing at all to a player who is not looking at the moment of the draw, it is invisible in a still (which is why the strip below runs three live draws instead of six photographs), and under reduced motion — where the game collapses every animation — it degrades to nothing whatsoever. That is a real gap the other three do not have.
