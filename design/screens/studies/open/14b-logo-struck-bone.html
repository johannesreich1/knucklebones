Take the neon out of the hero and give it back to the button. The name is answered literally: KNUCKLEBONES struck in ivory-to-bone metal, lit from above instead of from the side, held between two hairlines the width of the word. Both dice go to bone: still the app's own dice at 74px with their shipped tilts and shadow stack, --dc moved to ivory over a bone plate set behind the translucent glass the way study 56b built the launcher — no dice CSS is restated anywhere on this card. The duel pair survives only as two rakes of light entering from outside the box, the way study 56p put the two players on the lamps rather than on the die. Everything from the identity plate down keeps full neon, so the hero is the old object and the ladder below it is the live competition.

Cost. Hero 172 → 188px, +16 for the two rules and their air: the one direction on this slate that spends vertical space instead of returning it, and the homestack pays for it. Zero motion added; the primary button's glint is untouched. Zero localisation cost as drawn — the wordmark is the brand name and never translates — but see the first risk.

Risks, in order. (1) It argues with the product's own name for its build. Under a bone wordmark, "Neon Edition" reads as a leftover, so this is a copy decision hiding inside a type decision; changing that eyebrow is eleven strings and a brand call, not a CSS edit, and the card keeps the shipped string so the contradiction is visible rather than quietly resolved. (2) It deletes hue-coding from the one place a new player learns cyan=me / magenta=them. Two rakes are a much weaker signal than two coloured dice, and reading 1 is that argument put side by side rather than asserted. (3) Ivory and bone are already spoken for: --g-ivory and --g-bone are ladder-group hues (STONE, BONE, IVORY gate the mode pool), so the wordmark borrows ink that means "rank tier" three rows further down the same screen. Reading 2 answers the mud question — the ivory stays ivory over the aurora's worst magenta and does not turn yellow-green — but it cannot answer that one. (4) A 1px hairline is exactly the detail that survives on the desk and disappears on a phone at 30% brightness outdoors; .42 is the highest alpha this rule can take before it stops being a hairline and starts being a box, so there is no headroom left to buy that back with. (5) A bone pair at this value is now the lightest object on Home. It does not out-shout the PLAY gradient, which keeps all the saturation, but it does move the eye to the hero first — the opposite of what the online-first Home was arranged to do.
