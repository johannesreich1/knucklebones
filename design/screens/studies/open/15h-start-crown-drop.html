A8 · CROWN DROP

The name is standing when the app takes the screen, and the mark falls onto it. It does not bounce — it stops, and the wordmark answers with one pass of light along the gradient it already has. The crown lands on the name.

It is the only card that inverts the order. Everywhere else the mark leads and the type follows it up. 14e's word for what L5 does is CROWNING — the mark sits ON the type rather than beside or inside it — and a drop is that sentence said as motion. If the hero's whole justification is the relationship between two objects, the animation should be about the relationship, not about either object arriving. The impact belongs to the type, not the mark. A crown that rebounds is a ball, so the mark stops dead; what proves contact is the wordmark's reaction — one sweep of light travelling its gradient, left to right, once. That is the primary CTA's glint borrowed for a single frame, so the reaction introduces no new vocabulary and a player has already been trained to read it as "this thing is live". Order costs time. Type, then mark, then impact, then stack is four beats where every other card has two or three, and the PLAY button is the last thing to arrive at 620ms. This is the card most likely to be cut for feeling slow, and the reason it feels slow is the same reason it means something. Reduced motion loses the whole idea. A falling object is precisely what that setting refuses, and there is no quieter drop — so on that setting this card is a plain fade with no sweep, and the crowning is never stated. Watch it against A2. Both are about arrival; A2 says the mark you tapped is the mark you landed on, and A8 says the mark belongs on the name. They are not compatible — a mark that flew up from the launch frame cannot also fall from above — so the family has to pick one story.
