What it says: the face was rewritten, and here is the sum. NUDGE's whole rule is one pip; making the pips the subject is the only idea here whose picture IS the rule rather than a metaphor for it.
The wrap is devastating in exactly the right way. Going 5 → 6 one pip arrives; going 6 → 1, five pips drain out and a single one is left in the middle. Nothing has to warn the player that the wrap is a real cost — they watch it being paid.
It teaches the die. Players do not know that 3 → 4 moves the centre pip to the corners until they see it; after that the faces read as patterns rather than as numbers, which is worth something in a game made entirely of them.
Cost: for a few frames the face is between two layouts, and a die mid-rewrite is not a state the game has anywhere else — the same objection AN4 carries. It is also the quietest idea in the set: it happens inside a 56px box with no sound, no shake and nothing leaving the die, and a player who blinks has simply missed their one cast of the match.
