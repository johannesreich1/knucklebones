What it says: value moved. The two numbers this rune actually changes are already on screen, four pixels from the die it moves — and no other idea here touches them. Theirs goes 6 → 1 and yours 3 → 8 on the same frame, which is the only way to show the thing that makes PILFER strong: the swing is double the die, because the score difference moves at both ends. core/spells says exactly this out loud, in the comment that halves the machine's demand for one-sided runes.
The price is shown before the tap. A +5 tag on the die that would be taken turns "which one" into "what is it worth", and PILFER is the one rune where those are different questions — the top die of a column is not always the one you want.
Cheapest of the six by a distance. Two chips, one thread and a straight slide. No path to re-author per layout, nothing anchored outside a column, nothing that breaks when a half turns upside down.
Cost: it is arithmetic, not theft — the frames say "transfer complete" where the rune wants to say "taken". And a +5 tag is a real strength change, the same objection SUNDER's toll readout carries: it hands the player a comparison they would otherwise have to make themselves, across three columns, under a clock.
