What it says: how big the pile is — and size is the channel that most reliably survives peripheral vision at this scale, which is exactly what the refuted version could not claim.
The endgame stops being a guess. The shipped ceil(n / 24 × 4) shows a single shell for everything from six dice down to one, so the last five draws are pictorially identical. Here the final four are literal — three dice is three shells, one die is one flat tile — while the continuous ramp carries the twenty above them.
Cost — what the adversary took away. This was proposed as growing disorder: each shell rotated a little more as the bag emptied. That died on arithmetic — the shell has a 7.5px corner radius, so a few degrees moves the silhouette 0.39px and costs the crisp edge that makes it read as a die. Rotation is gone. What is left is honest but plain: 12px of height across the whole match is about 3.6px per checkpoint, which is real but undramatic, and a stack that is merely shorter has none of the flavour a "nearly empty bag" wants. It also changes the pile's height, which is the one dimension the 34×36 box has least of.
