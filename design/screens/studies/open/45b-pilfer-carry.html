What it says: it was carried, and set down. Everything this animation refuses to do is a rule: no impact, no burst, no shake on arrival, because the stolen die lands, it is not thrown — only a thrown die strikes (core/spells). A placement in this game always ends in a collision; this is the one that must not, and the silence is legible precisely because the rest of the game is loud.
The shadow is the argument. A shadow that grows under the die as it rises and shrinks as it settles is what separates carried over from slid across — and it costs one pseudo-element.
The hole stays open. Their column keeps the empty slot the die left, which is PILFER's real strategic content under COLUMN SHIELD (docs/SPELLS.md's known-too-strong pairing: the steal un-fills a nearly-full column and denies the shield). A stack that healed itself would hide that.
Cost: it is the quietest cast in the roster on a rune that costs a whole charge and takes something from a person sitting opposite you. There is a real risk it reads as underwhelming — or worse, as polite. The tuning knob is the lift height, and the honest test is a face-to-face game, not a screenshot.
