A6 · HALVES CLOSE

Two owners arrive separately and become one object. Each half is the shipped mark clipped down its middle, sliding in from its own side of the board, and the seam is not drawn — it is what is left where they meet.

It says the thing the logo is for. Every other card in this family animates the mark's arrival; this one animates its ARGUMENT — one die, two owners, joined on a seam. A player watching it once has been told what the game is before a single word of type has faded up. How it gets past the token. The appicon token is an image, so its plates are not addressable, and a study that needed the halves apart looked like it needed a hand-drawn logo. It does not: two copies of the same token, each clipped to one half with inset(), give independent halves made of the generator's own pixels. Nothing is redrawn, so this card cannot drift from tools/appicon.mjs — which is the rule the design library exists to keep. What the halves actually look like, captured rather than argued. The worry was that a clipped copy would fly as half of a SQUARE — margin and glow, no die. It does not: at 54px apart each half shows its plate, its three pips in a column, and the die's own inner highlight standing along the cut, so it reads as half a die from the first frame. The real weakness is the opposite one. Each half is legible enough to look like a finished object — a lit tile in its own right — so nothing in the picture says it is waiting for a partner, and the join reads as two things meeting rather than one thing reassembling. The pips are the only thing arguing otherwise: three in a column is not a face, and six is. Separation is a tuned number. 54px keeps the two halves reading as one object coming together. Past roughly 60 the eye stops predicting the join and the beat turns into a collision — two things hitting each other, which is a fight, not a duel. The blocker from 14e still applies, harder. The halves are cyan and magenta because the SVG says so, and here they are the only things on screen. A repointed pair breaks this card worse than any other in the family.
