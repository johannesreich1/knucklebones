{{die:6:p1:44}} {{die:1:p1:44}}{{die:1:p1:44}}{{die:2:p1:44}}{{die:3:p1:44}}{{die:4:p1:44}}{{die:5:p1:44}}{{die:6:p1:44}}{{die:1:p1:44}} 6 → 1
the wrap
What it says: one stop, always forward. The reel is the only idea in the set where the wrap needs no explanation and no special treatment: the 1 is simply the tile after the 6, and the player sees the order of the faces every time they cast. Nothing has to be learned twice.
It makes the bound visible. A player watching a strip of six understands immediately that this rune moves by one and cannot be aimed — which is the difference between a bounded rune and a wish.
Cost: it says "slot machine", and this is the one game that must never say that — the dice here are chosen, not spun, and the reel imports a fruit-machine grammar the rest of the game deliberately avoids. It also draws seven faces inside a 56px box, which means the two neighbours are visible during the roll: at speed that reads as motion blur, and paused it reads as three dice stacked in a window. Under reduced motion the roll collapses to its end state and the whole idea evaporates.
