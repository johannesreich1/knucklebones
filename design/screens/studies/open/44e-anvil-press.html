What it says: it was made to a spec. A press is the one image where the OUTPUT is decided before the machine closes — which is precisely ANVIL's promise and precisely where a re-roll (AN3) lies. The player already knows the new face; a press is an animation that agrees with them.
The substitution is hidden, on purpose. The face changes while the die is 6px tall, so there is no cross-fade to catch and no frame where two faces are half-visible. Of the six, this is the only one whose mechanics make the swap unfalsifiable rather than merely fast.
The chip is in the beat. 25 → 54 pops as the jaws open, so the thing the player spent a charge for lands at the moment their eye is already on the column. Every other idea leaves the score to arrive whenever the repaint gets to it.
Cost: the jaws need clearance above AND below the struck die, and the struck die can be in any of three slots — in the top slot the upper jaw comes down through the centre stage, in the bottom slot the lower jaw lands on the column chip. It is the least layout-tolerant idea of the six, and the fix (jaws that close from the sides instead) costs it the anvil-and-hammer reading it is trading on.
