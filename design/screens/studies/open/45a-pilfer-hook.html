What it says: you took it. The line is thrown from your own column, so the theft has a direction and an author — a die that merely drifted across would read as a gift, and PILFER is the roster's one aggressive rune (docs/SPELLS.md: stolen dice are not yet in this game's emotional vocabulary, which is exactly why the animation has to own it).
The mark is a bite, not a ring. Two corner brackets on the die that will be lifted: PILFER's target is one die, never the column, and a ring round the column would promise the wrong thing.
It survives the gold. The bracket sits outside the die's own border and only touches its corners, so it still reads on the enemy's multiplied pair — the case that beat the plain glow.
Cost: a line drawn between the two columns crosses the centre stage, where the die in play and the status line live. In portrait that is ~26px of occupied lane; in landscape the stage sits between the halves horizontally and the line would run through the rune rail. It is also the only idea here that needs a second element anchored to your column, so both rects have to be resolved at cast time, not one.
