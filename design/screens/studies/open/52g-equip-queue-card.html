Rune Ritual deals its own
What it says: this is what you are bringing. The queue is ranked's only calm second and the only screen whose whole job is to say hold on, so it is the one place a loadout can be shown without competing with a board. The rune sits there as the deck's own face-up card — the same .rdealt the reveal turns over a moment later — so choice, lock and reveal are three views of one object rather than a settings row that reappears as a chip mid-match.
Why it survives: it is the only idea on this board where the choice is made where it starts to matter and is then visibly carried into the duel. Three shipped objects, verbatim: the dealt card, the picker's .choice-lock, and matchmaking.trialLocked. And it answers the state the others have to argue about — a player with no runes at all — by not drawing.
Cost: the queue is the worst place in the app to ask for a decision. The median wait is a handful of seconds, so a tappable control turns waiting into fidgeting — and this one punishes the tap: changing the rune re-submits the join intent so the server can validate and snapshot the loadout, which means the search genuinely restarts and the clock goes back to 0:00. Tap at second six, lose your place, and the feature reads as a trap. Borrowing matchmaking.trialLocked also loads one sentence with two jobs — today it means "your Ritual rune is committed", here it would also mean "your equipped rune is committed" on a screen where nothing was chosen — and the frosted bar takes a third meaning below SILVER. That bar is near-opaque across its middle third, so it covers the icon it crosses — below SILVER the name under it is all that still says which rune is waiting. The card also competes for vertical band with the rolling die that is the screen's hero, and the message lane grows a line at the find because the app reserves no height for the longer sentence.
The state easiest to get wrong: below SILVER. The temptation is to hide the card until the choice does something, which teaches nothing. It stays face-up under the picker's own covered-choice banner instead, so the player spends every search watching their rune wait on a named group — louder than a dim icon on a screen they may never open. The picker row is not drawn here: it opens from the card, and EQ2 pictures it whole.
