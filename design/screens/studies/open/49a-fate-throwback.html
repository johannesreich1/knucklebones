What it says: you gave one back and took another. The rune's own glyph is a die leaving on a return arrow, and this is that glyph in motion — which means the picker, the sheet, the badge and the cast all draw the same idea instead of three of them drawing it and the fourth doing something else.
It cannot be mistaken for a re-roll. Two objects cross the stage, not one; the die that leaves is plainly a different die from the one that arrives, which is exactly the distinction between FATE and the roll that happens every turn anyway.
It makes the supply a place. Under LIMITED that place is on screen — the bag is 16px to the left of the die in play — so the throw goes toward the thing that is actually counting, and the count falling as the new die lands is the cost being paid where it happens.
Cost: it needs 92px of clear lane to the left of the stage, and that lane is exactly where the LIMITED bag lives; in landscape it is the whole 116px centre column (--land-lane), shared with the rune bar. In CLASSIC the die also flies toward nothing in particular, so the direction is a fiction that only becomes literal in one of the seven modes.
