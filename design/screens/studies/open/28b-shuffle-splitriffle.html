The hands: a tabled riffle, quoted whole. The fan is cut into two half-packets by alternation — odds gather left, evens right, and you watch cards 2 and 5 cross the felt mid-air to reach their halves. The packets square, tilt their inner corners together like two held halves under two thumbs, and release: one card at a time, R-L-R-L, each a quick low arc onto a growing centre stack while the packets visibly sink as they empty — the interleave is the shuffle. A tap squares the stack; it refans. Beats: FAN 420 → SPLIT 500 → TILT 260 → ZIP 1320 (six zips, one every ~190ms, ~290ms in the air) → SQUARE 240 → REFAN 680 → DEAL 560 → FLIP 520 — shuffle 3000ms, whole beat 4500ms (cap 4600). ZIP takes the full 1320 because six one-at-a-time hops need ~190ms apiece to stay countable; squeezed to the ~2.4s target the last three blur back into a block, which is today's exact complaint. Honest re-order: two redeals, like today's riffle+cut — redeal #1 (the riffled order) fires per card at the top of its SPLIT arc, redeal #2 (the settled order) at each card's ZIP peak. A card turns over its new rune at maximum motion, so the swap reads as that card being dealt into the stack, and the slot the draw reaches into is the one the last zip built. This card loops the shuffle twice — out through the interleave's slot swap and back home — because a CSS study cannot relabel runes the way redeal() does; in the app a single pass plus redeal()'s icon swaps achieves the same visible re-order. Cost vs S1: ~+0.5s over a plain retime and +1.6s over today; the six-rune offer is hidden for the ~2s the deck spends packed; and it is six per-card paths (split/hop/refan) where S1 keeps two mirrored blocks — more keyframes to tune, more ways to look wrong. Seam: still WAAPI in ui/runedeal.ts; restOf() unchanged; riffle()+cut() become four small builders (split/tilt/zip/refan) whose packet and stack poses are index functions like restOf; redeal() only moves its call sites.
