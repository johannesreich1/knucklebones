What it says: this is where the rune lives. The rail gets a floor, drawn with the board's own empty-seat values, so the card has somewhere to be and somewhere to be PUT. A played card lying square in its seat reads as stored rather than as a card that happens to be dark.
It protects the centre. The die is optically centred in a row the card hangs off; a seat means the card can grow, turn, lie down or be absent without the die moving a pixel — a failure already paid for once on the nameplate side.
Cost: it is one more piece of furniture in the busiest part of the table, and a seat drawn identically to the board's real slots a few pixels away is two things that look the same and mean different things.
Face-to-face: in portrait duo the card turns 180° with the rest of the centre — styles/game/seating.css already rotates #dieStage, .status and .timer for whoever is playing, and the card belongs in that selector. No new mechanism, one more name in a rule that exists.
