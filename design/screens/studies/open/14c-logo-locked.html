One silhouette, two owners, meeting on the launcher's cut. The pair stops facing each other across an 18px gap with a gold VS in it and interlocks by 17px — 23.6% of a die — where one white seam cuts both. Each die is clipped at that column rather than laid over the other, so the cyan outline runs into the seam and the magenta runs out of it: one continuous shape, folded on the cut, with the light in the fold.

Against today. The VS is deleted, so the hero drops to two colours and gold means only the ladder pill. The duel row narrows 211 → 127px, measured at the same die size in the strip above. The die falls 74 → 72 and the row's top margin 20 → 12 — one mass needs less air round it than two separated objects — so the hero measures 162 against 172 and hands 10px to the stack. The per-die 34px glow becomes one halo behind the joined mass, because a glow that crosses a cut gets cut with it. Zero motion, no new string: eyebrow and tagline are untouched and the wordmark never translates.

Risks. (1) THE PRECEDENT: 56a was two dice, mine in front and theirs behind, and it lost fifteen ways to 56b. But 56a lost at 29px, where an occluded back die is a smudge and the mark carries the brand alone; this is 72px under the wordmark, nothing is occluded — the cut is the pair's own mirror axis, so both owners lose the same slice — and what is borrowed is 56b's cut, not 56a's stacking. Read it as the rejected composition if you want to; the second strip row is where to decide. (2) Neither die is whole. The faces read because no pip crosses the cut (2.2px clear on the left face, 7.2px on the right), but that belongs to THESE faces at THIS overlap: if a hero face ever stops being fixed, the cut is re-measured or it slices a pip. (3) Two second implementations to retire first — the seam, which must become one shared class with tools/appicon.mjs' cut, and the halo, which is this object's own light rather than the app's --duel-die-outer-glow.
