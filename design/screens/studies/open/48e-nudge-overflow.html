Both directions, the same idea: the ordinary tick, and the wrap that pays for it
{{die:5:p1:44}} {{die:6:p1:44}} 5 → 6
the tick
{{die:6:p1:44}} {{die:1:p1:44}} 6 → 1
the wrap
What it says: the six is full. The wrap is the rune's price, and this is the only idea that charges it out loud — a seventh pip that will not fit, a face that floods, five pips on the floor. A player who has seen it once will hesitate over a 6 forever, which is exactly the hesitation the balance depends on.
It keeps the ordinary case cheap. Five casts out of six are a small rise and a new face; the drama is spent entirely on the case that deserves it, which is the opposite of the usual mistake of animating everything equally.
The red is already in the palette — --red is the multiplier fallback, a raw token that never follows the duel pair, so a player who has picked red dice still reads this as heat rather than as their own colour.
Cost: it dramatises a legal, deliberate, and sometimes CORRECT cast as a failure. Wrapping a 6 to a 1 is occasionally the right move — a 1 into a column full of 1s, or a 6 you cannot afford to place — and an animation that treats it as an accident tells the player they made a mistake when they did not. It is also the one idea here that needs a shake, and shake is the game's word for destruction.
