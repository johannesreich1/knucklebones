Nothing travels. The launch frame's flat night gives way to the app's own aurora, and the mark comes into focus out of it — blurred and desaturated, then sharp and lit. The room arrives, then what is in it.

The only card here with no transform. Nothing moves, so nothing can read as a thing being fetched. That is the argument: card 35 refuses text on the launch frame because text reads as slow, and travel reads as slow for the same reason. A screen that comes into focus is not making a promise it has to keep. It uses the ground the app already has. The aurora is .bgfx, on every screen in the product, so this beat introduces no new visual vocabulary at all — it simply turns on the thing that is always there. That is why it is the cheapest to defend in review and the hardest to get credit for. The blur is the whole cost. A 7px blur on a 96px image is a filter pass on the first painted frame of the app, and it is the one number here that has to be measured rather than argued: too little and nothing happened, too much and the die stops being a six before it resolves. 7px was set against the pip spacing — the face must be unreadable but the object must still be a die. Fair objection: did anything happen? Held next to A3's unwind or A4's lamps this looks like a fade, and a fade is what boot does today when a webview paints. If the answer to "what did the animation say" is "the app is here", A5 said it and nothing else. Some reviewers will call that restraint and some will call it nothing; both readings are correct. Reduced motion is a plain fade — a resolving blur still reads as instability, so it degrades to the state change alone.
