What it says: a column was repaired. ANVIL is aimed at a column, priced by a column (full ones only), and paid out in a column's score — 25 to 54 here, from one die. Every other idea in this set animates a die and lets the column follow silently; this one puts the beat where the value is.
It is the only one that can show a TRIPLE arriving. [6,1,6] becoming [6,6,6] is not "one die changed", it is three dice becoming a set — and the app already has a look for that, .die.m3's orange with its slow hot pulse. Landing the three faces together and letting the column's own glow carry the beat hands the animation off to a state the board keeps for the rest of the game.
The mark keeps the promise cheaply. The column body is lit, and the chosen die gets a 10px notch under it — enough to answer "which one", small enough that the column stays the subject.
Cost: it is the least specific about the moment of change. The face swaps inside a squash that lasts six frames, and a player watching the column glow may not register that a 1 became a 6 at all — they will see the number change and take it on trust. That is fine for a repair and wrong for a rune whose whole appeal is you chose which die. Pairs naturally with a louder die-level mark (AN1's hammer, AN4's pips) rather than standing alone.
