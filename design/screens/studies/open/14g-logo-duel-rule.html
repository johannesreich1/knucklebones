The picture becomes a line, and the hero gives back 73 pixels. The two 74px dice and the gold VS are deleted. The wordmark goes to plain white at 30px/.08em — the shared gradient put cyan on the K, magenta on the S and plain white across the eight letters between them — and the duel is carried by a 2px rule the exact width of the word: the player's hue running in from the left, the opponent's from the right, meeting at one white node where the VS used to shout. Measured, not estimated: the hero goes 172px → 99px (10 eyebrow + 4 + [35 word + 10 + 2 rule + 12] + 4 + 10 tagline + 12), so 73px come back to the stack's budget on the app's own centred axis. The brand gets BIGGER while the hero gets shorter: 236px painted today, 258px here, 255px trimmed. Zero motion, and that is a rule, not an omission: a glowing 2px bar spanning a title on the first screen after launch is the shape of a progress bar, and any sweep or pulse turns a possible misreading into a certain one. Zero localisation cost — the rule is sized off the wordmark, which never translates, and the eyebrow and tagline (re-set as one 9px/.30em pair) sit outside it. The node is the only part that cannot scale linearly — .2em floored at 4px, so 6px at the hero and 4px from a 170px rule down, still twice the line's own height.

Risks, honestly. One: Home loses its only picture. Two glowing dice say "dice game" before a word is read; a hairline does not, and this is the first screen a new player sees and the frame most store screenshots use. Two: the precedent. Launcher study 56n took the die out of the mark and lost, which is evidence the owner wants the object present rather than abstracted. This is not that argument: 56n asked an abstraction to carry the whole brand alone at 29px, where nothing else is on screen; here the die is still the launcher, the avatar, the loader and every board in the product, and the hero is a title, not a tile. If that distinction does not hold, this direction should lose for the same reason 56n did. Three: "it is static so it is not mistaken for something else" is a thin reason for an element to exist — the rule has to earn its 2px by reading as a duel, and if it only reads as a divider, the honest version of this card is the wordmark alone.
