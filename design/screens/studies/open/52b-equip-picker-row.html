What it says: you already know this control. The offline rune picker — label, icon row, NONE first, the sentence that names the pick — moves onto the Profile under a collection grid left exactly as it is. Nothing new is drawn for the first rune either: the row arrives with it already lit and already named, which is what told, not asked looks like when the answer is a control you can see is set. NONE sits first, so un-equipping is one tap on a slice, not a hatch to find.
Why it survives: no new visual vocabulary at all. Selected, none, locked-per-item, gated-as-a-whole and gated-for-Ritual are already drawn in picker.css, already translated, and already known from offline setup, so the SILVER gate costs one string in the existing runeTrial.lock voice. It also leaves shell.ts:96-98 nearly honest: the collection above stays a collection.
Cost — the most furniture of the eight. Two rune rosters on one screen, and a player may fairly read them as one control shown twice. The row does not fit: seven 52px buttons and six 9px gaps are 418px against a measured 350px column, so it lands 5 + 2 — the lonely tail picker.css's own comment was written to prevent. The meaning is borrowed imperfectly too: offline a named pick is a symmetric deal handing both seats the same rune, while this is one seat's loadout. RANDOM and RANDOM x2 have to go, and both that subset and the per-item padlocks need arguments pickerButtons does not have.
The state easiest to get wrong: waiting. The banner is the right object, but the Ritual lock it is borrowed from also hides the sentence, and copying that would say the wrong thing — below SILVER the choice is made and saved and merely not in play, so its name must stay readable. Two covers, two meanings, one element: the second and third cards above differ in exactly that line. A demotion re-deals the banner over a selection nobody touched.
