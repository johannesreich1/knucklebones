OPEN STUDY · MARK 08 · THE BEAT

THE STACKED BONES

The drawing is settled; only its gesture is in question. The back bone is an OPEN path — drawn along the edges the front bone does not hide, its two ends stopping exactly on the front bone's top and right edges. That is what sells the depth standing still, and it is why moving the front bone alone left an unfinished rectangle behind.

NOW — the fault The front bone lifts and the back bone is left with two loose ends hanging in the middle of the mark: a stub below its top-left corner, and a bottom edge that stops in mid-air. Nothing is wrong with the timing. Watch it against any of the six below.
NOW S1 S2 S3 S4 S5 S6

Same contract as the parent study — 9.2s, two turns of the button's own --primary-glint-cycle, about two seconds of motion and then seven still, every gesture a ROUND TRIP that starts and ends on the resting frame. Only the gesture changes below.

S1PRESSMOVES AS ONE

Both bones move as ONE. The pair compresses a little and springs back, the way a stack of chips does when a thumb comes off it.

BEAT The stack presses down and springs back.

COST Nothing separates, so nothing can come apart — but nothing reads as two dice either.
S2ROCKMOVES AS ONE

The pair tips on its bottom-left corner and rights itself. Weight and a table, in one gesture, with the two bones still locked together.

BEAT The stack tips on its corner and rights itself.

COST A rotation on a 25px mark is the easiest thing to make look like a wobbling bug.
S3STAMPMOVES AS ONE

The pair lands: a drop with a squash on impact and a small rebound. The loudest of the six and the one that most says THROWN.

BEAT The pair drops, squashes on impact, and rebounds.

COST Squash and stretch on a geometric mark can read as a rendering glitch rather than as weight.
S4PIP

The dice never move at all. Only the front bone's pip swells and settles — the mark breathes rather than acts.

BEAT The front pip swells once and settles.

COST The quietest by far. On a 25px button it may read as no animation at all.
S5LIFTSEPARATES

The front bone is picked UP off the stack and set back down. The only gesture that shows the two as separable objects.

BEAT The front bone lifts off the stack and settles back onto it.

COST Only works because the back bone is masked by the front one; drawn as a plain outline it comes apart the instant it moves.
S6DEALSEPARATES

The front bone slides DOWN-LEFT along the stack's own diagonal and back, the way a card is dealt off the top of a deck.

BEAT The front bone slides off the stack and returns.

COST Same masking cost as LIFT, and a slide is easier to mistake for the mark simply drifting.
The four marked MOVES AS ONE wrap both bones in a single group and gesture with it, so the open path and the shape that hides its ends never come apart — there is no seam to get wrong. The two marked SEPARATES pay for the separation with one extra stroke, .pmjoin, the corner the front bone normally hides; it sits at zero opacity at rest and is only visible while the bone is genuinely off the stack. If one of those two wins, the shipped glyph should use a mask rather than a second path, so the occlusion is correct for any motion instead of for one.
