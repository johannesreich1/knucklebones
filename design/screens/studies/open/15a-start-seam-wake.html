A1 · SEAM WAKE

The mark is already in place when the webview takes the screen — grey, unlit, the object without its owners. The seam strikes as a single lit line, then the light spreads outward and the two plates take their hues. The split is stated before the colour is.

What it says. The seam is the mark's entire argument — one die, two owners, a single cut — and this is the only card in the family that shows the cut BEFORE it shows the colour. A player who never thinks about the icon still gets told, in 620ms, that this thing is divided. How it is built. Two copies of the shipped appicon token, stacked and identical: the under copy grayscaled and dimmed, the over copy clipped to nothing and opened from the centre out. Nothing is redrawn, so the study cannot drift from tools/appicon.mjs; and because the reveal is a clip-path on the real image, the seam that appears IS the generator's seam, not a line this card invented on top of it. What it costs. Two <img> of the same data URI — the second is free from cache, but it is a second 512px SVG decode at boot, on the one frame where the app has the least budget. Measure before shipping: if the decode lands late the mark pops instead of waking, which is worse than the hard cut it replaced. Where it is weak. A grayscale mark held for 200ms on a dark ground is nearly invisible, so the beat can read as the mark ARRIVING at 17% rather than waking — the opposite of the intent. The unlit copy may need to sit brighter than the eye wants (brightness .34 here) purely so the wake reads as a change of state. Against the hard cut. Boot today has no motion at all and nobody has complained. Anything here has to earn 620ms on the app's slowest frame.
