A2 · LAND AND SETTLE

The mark the OS already painted does not disappear and get replaced — it moves. It rises from the centre of the launch frame to the top of the page and settles at hero size, and the type and the stack rise to meet it. Nothing appears; something arrives.

Its premise went into the product on 2026-09-03, before the motion did. The card was drawn to argue one thing — that the launch storyboard should carry the split die rather than the cyan five — and that argument was strong enough to act on by itself: tools/splash.mjs now renders splitDieIconSVG. Boot no longer shows two marks in half a second, and every other card in this family got the benefit for free, because the launch frame they all picture comes from the same generator. What that bought. Continuity is the only thing a start animation can honestly sell. A player who taps a split-die tile, sees a split die on the frame, and lands on a split-die hero has been shown one app three times. The other cards decorate the moment between frame and page; this one removed the reason the moment looked wrong. What is left to decide is the motion, and it is thin. Three pixels of scale and a rise. Held against A3's unwind or A6's closing halves this is nearly nothing, and that is the honest case for it: the mark has already done its work on the tile, and a hero that performs on arrival is a hero asking for attention it was just given. The travel is a real liability. 224px is the gap between the stage's centre and the mark's seat AT THIS CARD'S HEIGHT — measured here, and wrong everywhere else. In the app that distance is a function of the device, the safe area and the stack, so it cannot be a constant: it has to be read from layout at boot, on the frame with the least budget, or the mark lands somewhere it does not belong. The first draft of this card proved the point by hard-coding 158 and dropping the die on the wordmark. Against the hard cut. 660ms, and the PLAY button waits all of it.
