A2 · LAND AND SETTLE

The mark the OS already painted does not disappear and get replaced — it moves. It rises from the centre of the launch frame to the top of the page and settles at hero size, and the type and the stack rise to meet it. Nothing appears; something arrives.

The real proposal is the substitution, not the motion. This card only works if the launch storyboard renders the SPLIT die instead of the splash five. Today tools/splash.mjs draws a single cyan five and tools/appicon.mjs draws the split die, so boot already shows two different marks in half a second — a continuity break nobody animated their way out of. Make them one object and the whole beat becomes a 3px change of scale, which is why this is the quietest card in the family. What it buys. Continuity is the only thing a start animation can honestly sell. A player who taps a split-die tile, sees a split die on the frame, and lands on a split-die hero has been shown one app three times. Every other card here decorates the seam between two marks; this one removes the seam. What it costs. A change to a shipped generator and to every native rendition it writes, plus a new launch storyboard — and the splash five is not nothing: it is the app's oldest mark and the only place the single cyan die still appears at size. Card 35 argues the launch frame should carry no text because text reads as slow; this argues it should carry the same die for the same reason. The measured distance is a liability. The flying copy travels −158px, which is the gap between the stage's centre and the hero's seat AT THIS CARD'S HEIGHT. In the app that distance is a function of the device, the safe area and the stack, so it cannot be a constant — it has to be read from layout at boot, on the frame with the least budget, or the mark lands in the wrong place on a tall phone. Against the hard cut. 660ms is the longest beat in this family, and it delays the PLAY button by all of it.
