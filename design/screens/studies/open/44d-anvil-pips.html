What it says: the face was rewritten in place. The shell never moves a pixel — no squash, no jump, no spin — so the one sentence of ANVIL's rule people get wrong (nothing moves, nothing is destroyed, the column keeps its height) is the one thing this animation physically cannot contradict.
It is the only idea that changes the thing that actually changed. A die's face IS its pips; every other idea here animates the die and swaps the face inside the noise. Draining and refilling makes the transformation legible frame by frame, and it works identically at any board size because it has no geometry outside the die.
The mark is also the pips — the chosen die's pips take the forge hue and breathe, while its border and slot stay exactly as they were. Nothing is added to the board, so nothing can collide with the chip row or the centre stage.
Cost: for a few frames the die has no pips, and a blank die already means something in this game — the LIMITED bag draws face-down dice as a pip-less shell on purpose. The darkening wash is there to keep those frames from reading as "face-down", and whether it is enough is a thing to look at on a real phone, not in a card. It is also the least dramatic of the six: a cast that costs the player their only charge may want more than a tidy substitution.
