The mark arrives with no light on it. Two lamps travel in from opposite edges in the player's own two hues, meet on the seam, and hand off to the glow that stays. The duel arrives before the game does — from my side and from theirs.

It animates the only part of the hero that is not a picture. 14e's blocker is that splitDieIconSVG writes its own cyan and magenta onto the SVG root, so the die cannot follow a repointed pair. The re-light glow can: it is two CSS drop-shadows outside the image, reading live --p1-rgb and --p2-rgb. Everything that moves in this card is on that side of the line, so this is the one start animation that is correct for every player today. And that is also the cruelty. A player who repointed to violet-and-green watches THEIR two lamps sweep in and light a die that is still cyan and magenta. The animation makes the mismatch worse by drawing attention to exactly the thing that is wrong — 14e's strip row 2 is that failure standing still; this is it in motion. Ship this only after the split leaves tools/appicon.mjs and becomes live DOM, and then it is the strongest card here. The direction carries meaning. Mine comes from the left and theirs from the right, which is the board's own geometry (player index is identity — 1 is cyan and mine, 0 is magenta and theirs). Reverse it and the animation is still pretty and now says something false. mix-blend-mode: screen is the risk. Two blended layers over the aurora at boot is the most expensive thing in this family, on the frame with the least budget. If it stutters the lamps read as a dropped frame rather than a sweep. Measure on the slowest device before defending the effect. Reduced motion degrades honestly here — the travel goes, the settled glow stays, and the mark still ends lit in the player's colours.
