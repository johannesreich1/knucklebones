The hands: a dealer's spring flourish — the fan squares up, collapses into the left hand, and the cards leap the gap one at a time in high arcs into the right (two to three airborne at once), then spring straight back in the other order before the deck is laid back down as the fan. Every card visibly travels; nothing teleports, and the top card of each stack is always the next to fly. Beats (ms): FAN 340 · SQUARE 200 · GATHER 420 · CASCADE→ 840 · BREATH 160 · CASCADE← 840 · REFAN 540 · DEAL 560 · FLIP 520 — shuffle portion 3000 (today 1250), whole beat 4420 (today 2890). Honest re-order: redeal() moves from mid-beat to each card's landing thock — the first cascade writes the intermediate order across six spaced touchdowns (16.1–21.6% of the loop here), the return cascade writes settled across six more (55.1–60.6%), so every icon swap rides a card that just arrived somewhere new, and the slot the draw reaches into was fixed by the last arc the player watched. This card loops the flourish twice — one cascade out to a fan that rests with every rune in a new slot, and a mirrored cascade back home — because a CSS study cannot relabel runes the way redeal() does; in the app a single pass plus redeal()'s icon swaps achieves the same visible re-order. Cost vs S1: the longest shuffle of the set, at the 3.0s cap, and the busiest — twelve arcs at ~16 keyframe segments per card against the riffle's five; the one-at-a-time gesture is maximally legible ("every card moved" is literally shown) but repeats the same hop twelve times, so it trades suspense for showmanship and risks reading mechanical by the hundredth deal.
