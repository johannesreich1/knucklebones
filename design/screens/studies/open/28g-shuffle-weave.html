The hands: a faro weave. The fan is squared into one stack, the top half is lifted off to the right, both packets seat corner-to-corner — and then telescope INTO each other, one card at a time in the order 4,1,5,2,6,3 — the in-faro, the cut packet's bottom card leading, so every rune lands in a new slot of the fan. The meshed deck bows into a bridge (adjacent layers tilted apart — the frame where the alternation is read), cascades flat bottom-to-top, breathes once, and refans. Beats: FAN 380 → GATHER 360 → SPLIT 270 → ALIGN 135 → WEAVE 855 → BRIDGE 270 → CASCADE 360 → BREATH 135 → REFAN 495 → DEAL 560 → FLIP 520; shuffle 2880 ms, whole beat 4340 ms (today 1250 / 2900). Honest re-order: redeal() lands twice — the mid order as each card SEATS in the mesh (its corner index is buried by the next arrival, so the swap reads as that card being woven into that slot), and the settled order as each card LEAVES the stack at its refan launch, today's carries-a-new-rune-back-into-the-fan moment kept intact. The draw then reaches into a slot the weave chose. This card loops the shuffle twice — out through the in-faro's permutation to a held re-ordered fan, then back home through the unweave — because a CSS study cannot relabel runes the way redeal() does; in the app a single pass plus redeal()'s icon swaps achieves the same visible re-order. Cost vs the S1 retime: ~1.6 s more card handling than today and the longest shuffle of the set; six bespoke per-card tracks instead of two mirrored block gestures, so it is the most implementation surface; the precision reads as skilled hands but cooler than a riffle — and the alternation lives in the entry stagger, so compressing WEAVE under ~800 ms re-creates the exact two-blocks failure this redesign exists to fix.
