What it says: these dice are armoured — the protection is a property of each die, not a box drawn round three of them. Two rims and a slow catch of light do the whole job, and every pixel of it lands inside the die's own footprint: .col.legal's dashed ring sits at −4px and ROW MULTIPLY's bracket at −3px, and this option never goes out there to argue with either. That also makes it the one of the six that survives landscape unchanged, where the board is transposed and the gutters are the tightest thing on screen. The drop is cladding, not blocking: the die falls and settles exactly as it does into an unguarded column — a ward eats a STRIKE, never your own placement — and only then does the plate walk across it behind a glint. Absorb flashes the struck plate white, runs the discharge out along the seams, and leaves that plate grey; a spent ward is a dead plate, so the charge reads without a number. Cost: this is the quietest option of the six, and quiet is the failure mode these studies were called to fix — 1px of hue on a die that already carries a 1px border in its owner's colour lifts the mark barely clear of the die itself, and it is the hardest of the six to spot from arm's length or on a 360-wide screen. The gold rim is also the ×2 heat's ink: a shielded column of .m2 dice wears gold on gold and the two marks blur. And a ward on an empty column plates nothing at all — three hairline seats is the entire mark, in exactly the state a defensive player casts into.
