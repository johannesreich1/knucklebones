What it says: the metal was reworked. The rune is a forge; this is the only idea that spends its frames on the material rather than on a tool. The die goes hot, goes soft, and comes back — which is a recast, described exactly.
The quietest mark before the tap. No badge, no bracket, no hammer: the chosen die is simply already glowing. Nothing is added to the board's geometry, so nothing has to be found room for — the cheapest of the six to fit at any board size, in any orientation.
It uses the app's own heat. ANVIL's hue is forge-red on purpose (docs/SPELLS.md: the metal worked, not the flame), and the same white-out this plays through is what .die.m3 already does at a third of the intensity — so the finished [6,6,6] cooling into m3's own orange is a continuation, not a hand-off.
Cost: "already hot" reads as danger elsewhere in this game — a die about to be destroyed is exactly what SUNDER's ember says. Two runes using heat to mean opposite things (yours is about to improve / theirs is about to die) is a real collision, and the hue gap between ember #ff9d66 and forge #ff7591 may not be wide enough to carry it.
