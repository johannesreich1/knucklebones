What it says: the column is sealed — a mark drawn around it, not a wash laid over it. The stroke sits outside the slots, so it costs the dice nothing: no tint, no lost pip contrast. It beats today's ring for the opposite reason a glow would — it moves. A 12% halo is invisible on a lit board; a dash that crawls is not, even at 1.7px. The ticks carry the count: one per protected row, so a full shield reads as three-of-three without a number. The drop runs one fast lap and settles — your die is never stopped, because a ward eats a STRIKE, not your placement. The absorb is the argument: the ticks burn out from the point of impact and the line gutters, and then the shield redraws itself from the break while the ward stays open and its rune goes out. One mechanism, two rules, no legend. Cost: it is the thinnest proposal of the six and the first to lose — to a small phone, a bright room, or a busy board behind it; the crawl reads as "loading" if it is ever run faster than this; and it is the only one that animates for the entire match, so with REDUCED motion it collapses to a static dashed ring and gives back most of what it was for.
