What it says: the column is capped, not outlined — the guard stands over its mouth, the open end a die is thrown into, which is the most literal thing a shield can be on this board: something between the column and what is coming at it. The mouth is always the end facing the centre stage, so the dome arcs up over the top of a near-half column and down under the bottom of a far-half one — the two domes face each other across the die stage, which is exactly the geometry of the table. The drop: your die passes straight through. The dome gives 5%, the hexes nearest the crossing light and fade outward, the wake runs off both feet — and the die's speed never changes, so the animation cannot tell the lie that a ward refuses your own placement. Nothing it does is ever in the die's way. The absorb: the lattice draws itself all at once, the rim whitens, the enemy die stops dead on the crown and is thrown back the way it came; then a ward's dome goes out from the feet up, because the mark is spent — a shield's would simply settle. Cost: it is the only one of the six that costs the column no width — nothing in the gutters, nothing over a die, pip contrast untouched — but it buys that with height: ~30px of clear lane over every mouth, and on the near half that lane is where the chip strip already sits, 4px clear. Either both strips move (about 50px out of a 640px screen, and the board is tightest on a 360 phone) or the dome tucks behind the chip and loses the crown that carries it. It also marks the mouth and not the column: a full shielded column and a one-die warded column wear an identical dome, so it says protected without ever saying how much. And it is the only option with an axis — in landscape the boards sit side by side and the columns run sideways, so the dome has to rotate onto the inner end with them. A ring does not care which way the board is turned.
