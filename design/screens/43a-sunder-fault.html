What it says: the strike is one break, already begun. Because every seam runs at the same angle, the eye joins them into a single fault crossing the enemy half — which is the only thing about SUNDER that is hard to picture: that it is one placement reaching everywhere, not three separate hits.
The die in hand is the origin, not a copy: its seam is the wide molten one and the board's are hairlines off it. That ranks the two halves of the animation without a label.
It survives the gold. The seam is a white core with an ember shoulder, drawn over the die and cut by a dark shadow line — so it still reads on a die already wearing the multiplier's gold border and bloom, which is where the current ember glow disappears entirely.
Cost: it is a line across the face, and on a 3 or a 5 it lands close to the middle pip.
